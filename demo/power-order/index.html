Fleet build

The 5 V rail, then the printed shell it lives in. One brick feeds eight fused USB-C sockets. Every lead is USB-C on both ends. The shell holds that board, the 1420, the four blades, and the two bare screens. Switch ports leave through the right rear opening. The mains plug leaves through the left one.

Parts

Quantities cover four blades, two screens, and two fan leads. Hook-up wire is 1 m red and 1 m black, 22 AWG.

5 V brick

Mean Well GST60A05-P1J

Sealed 5 V, 6 A, 30 W. The barrel in the photo is 5.5 × 2.1 mm, center positive. The mains cord is not in the box.

Mains lead

BS 1363 to IEC C13, 1 m

Wall end is the UK plug. Brick end is a right-angle C13. The brick sits on the left of the switch, long axis front to back, and the inlet faces the rear openings.

Buy 1

Barrel socket

5.5 × 2.1 mm, center positive

This is the hole the brick plugs into. Order the panel version with solder tags, so it can be wired to the stripboard. Center pin is +5 V.

Buy 8

USB-C sockets

USB-C female, through-hole, plus and minus

A USB-C plug goes into each one. Buy receptacles with solder pins, not a cable-end plug. Solder only plus and minus. Four blades, two screens, two lid fans. The fans themselves come later. There is no intake fan.

Buy 6

Blade and fan fuses

Bourns MF-R110

Resettable fuse, 1.1 A hold, two leads, the small round body. One each for N1–N4 and the two lid-fan sockets. The photo shows the family; match the printed hold current.

Buy 2

Screen fuses

Bourns MF-R185

Same shape, higher hold current: 1.85 A. One for vigía, one for cabildo. MF-R200 is the stand-in if R185 is out of stock.

Buy 1

Stripboard

Any small piece, 0.1 inch pitch

Copper strips are on the reverse of this face. The board in the shell is 70 × 90 mm, which fits the jack, eight fuses, and eight USB-C sockets.

Buy 6

C-to-C leads

USB 2.0, USB-C male both ends

Four at 0.5 m for the blades, two at 1 m for vigía and cabildo. Two more, later, one for each 50 mm lid fan. Ordinary data cables, not a hair-thin charge lead. Keep the C-to-C cables you already have for the laptop.

Schematic

Every fuse is in the positive lead only. Each USB-C socket on this board is plus and minus. Nothing else is soldered there.

Wiring diagram

Solder it like this. The × on each fuse is a cut in the copper strip between its two legs. Red is +5 V. Black is ground.

Blade harness

Four short wires, soldered, on every blade. Same nets on N1–N4. Colors here only tell the nets apart. Power lands on the service row. USB data lands on the expansion row, pins 19 and 20. CC and SBU holes stay empty.

The shell

Inside 242 × 229 × 100 mm. The rear face sits on the switch port face, so there is no cable gutter. The floor is flat, with vents under the switch. The GST60A is left of the switch. The 70 × 90 mm fuse board is on the floor in front of the switch. A 3.2 mm plate taped to the switch top holds the four bare blades and their USB-C breakouts. A 45 mm LED slot is centered high on the front face. Outside 247 × 236 × 102 mm. PETG, 0.2 mm layers, 3 perimeters, 20% infill, no supports. The mesh is hardware/stl/shell.stl: tub, lid, front, and rear laid out side by side.

The board drawing matches the schematic: green stripboard, fuse in the positive lead only, USB-C sockets wired as plus and minus. Part photos: GST60A from Mean Well. UK plug, C13, barrel socket, fuses, stripboard, and the USB connectors are Wikimedia Commons files.

Cut the copper strip between each fuse’s legs. A blade takes 5 V from this board or from the laptop, never both. The 1420 stays on its own mains lead.

The shell drawings match hardware/shell.scad. The mesh is hardware/stl/shell.stl. The picture above them is generated. Outside 247 × 236 × 102 mm, so the tub fits a 256 mm bed. Print tub, lid, front, and rear as four pieces.
